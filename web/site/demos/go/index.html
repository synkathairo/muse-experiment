9×9 Go, played by a tiny neural net

This page runs a real 9×9 Go engine in your browser via WebAssembly, compiled from the weiqi-engine Rust code — modified Tromp–Taylor rules (captures, suicide ban, positional superko, two-pass end, area scoring with 7.5 komi) plus a hand-rolled 130k-parameter CNN forward pass. Rules. Modified Tromp–Taylor: Black moves first, then the sides alternate; a turn is a stone placement or a pass, and groups left with no liberties are captured. Suicide is banned unless it captures, and no earlier board position may ever repeat (positional superko). Two consecutive passes end the game. Scoring: every stone on the board counts as alive, plus empty points fully surrounded by one color; White gets 7.5 komi. There is no dead-stone marking — capture dead groups before the final passes. Two deliberate deviations from pure Tromp–Taylor: suicide is banned outright (pure TT lets it self-clear) and komi is fixed at 7.5 (pure TT leaves it to player agreement).

Honest caveats. These checkpoints are from a clean 3M-step self-play run on Apple Silicon (M1/MPS) with corrected training semantics — the buggy CPU pilot's weights are gone. At 3M the net scores 10–22 against GNU Go levels 1–10, but the strength is lopsided: 8–8 as White, 2–14 as Black. Opening play going first is the known weakness; more steps alone don't fix it. They exist so you can watch learning happen, and the numbers above are the measured reality, not a strength claim.

How the training works is written up in weiqi/theory.md — search-free PPO self-play, a different beast from AlphaZero's MCTS.
